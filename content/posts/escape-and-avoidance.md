Escape and avoidance is sometimes called Traditional training. First the dog learns to *escape* light pressure by doing the behavior. Then they learn to *avoid* it completely by doing the behavior before the pressure ever shows up. The pressure I use most is leash pressure with a pinch collar.

{{< youtube SnMAGTHxG2Y >}}

That's four sessions of force fetch with Griz. The point isn't the fetch. It's teaching a dog to handle stress and to listen when there's no reward on the table.

## Two rules

- **The pressure must always be escapable.** Pressure a dog can't escape leads to fear and learned helplessness.
- **Start low.** Begin below the level that changes behavior, and only go up until you see results.

I don't hit, alpha roll, yell, or use social pressure. They're unclear, too intense, and give the dog no way out.

## Yes, it's stressful at first

When it's new you'll see ears back, a stiff or tucked tail, and plenty of resistance. As the novelty wears off, so do the fear and anxiety. Done right, getting it right starts to feel good to the dog. Done wrong, it can traumatize them, and without food and play a dog can end up looking suppressed. So layer the fun back in.
