Enrichment is time when dogs get to be dogs. Off leash, smelling what they want to smell. Long walks together where you get to be you and I get to be me, and we do that together, without structure.

But it's more than walks. If I could hand your dog the perfect human with the perfect schedule, here's what that human would give them.

| Give your dog | What that looks like |
| -- | -- |

## My dogs' day

{{< timeline >}}
1. **Wake up.** Training collars on, usually the e-collar, sometimes a 10-15' long line. We walk 2 miles. Some of it is training. Most of it is just walking. *[1 Be a dog](#1-be-a-dog) [3 Play](#3-play)*
2. **I go to work.**
3. **I get home.** They meet me at the gate and sit patiently while I greet them one at a time. Then Denali runs ahead and Griz heels by the truck as we drive in. *[1 Be a dog](#1-be-a-dog)*
4. **Out of the truck.** Five minutes of food or play training with each dog. Then I go greet the human family members, who understand how to be patient and wait their turn. *[3 Play](#3-play)*
5. **Until dinner.** We don't do anything.
6. **Before dinner.** Scent games or stress training. *[4 Dog jobs](#4-dog-jobs) [8 Handle stress](#8-handle-stress)*
7. **Dinner, plus an hour.** They eat, then each dog gets alone time in the kennel. *[2 Eat well](#2-eat-well) [5 Alone time](#5-alone-time)*
8. **After that.** They relax when I do, until it's time to train or go to bed. *[6 Relax](#6-relax) [7 Chill](#7-chill)*
9. **Before bed.** At least one scent game and one stress exercise for each dog. Maybe some engagement with play, when the dogs talk me into it. Which they often do. *[4 Dog jobs](#4-dog-jobs) [8 Handle stress](#8-handle-stress) [3 Play](#3-play)*

## 1. Be a dog

Go for a long walk and let them smell what they want to smell. Here's one of ours by a lake. Both dogs wear e-collars and I have the remote ready, but I never use it.

{{< youtube cFSE0V6iDTQ >}}

- **Off leash means 100%.** Only do it when you're 100% sure of your verbal control.
- **An [e-collar](/posts/e-collar/) helps you get to 100%.** If your dog goes to do something inappropriate or dangerous, [negative reinforcement](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/) gets them through it, even against strong distractions or something you haven't trained for.
- **I use one in public.** It's that tool, and it's an example for others.
- **Not there yet?** Use a long line. They still get to sniff.

## 2. Eat well

A healthy diet is enrichment too. [Here's what my dogs eat](/posts/what-my-dogs-eat/).

{{< youtube gZd-anFsCvc >}}

How often I feed depends on the dog's age.

| Dog | Meals | Why I do it |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Growing | Several a day | They absorb calories better when it's spread out |
| Full grown | One, about every 24 hours | Food drive, and a settled stomach |

Predators are good at fasting, better than us. My thinking is that time between meals gives the gut biome a chance to reset and the bacteria time to even out. It's why vets have you fast a dog with an upset stomach for 24 hours, then go to chicken and rice. My dogs get that fast between every meal, and they show up hungry for training.

After dinner I wait an hour before we do anything, because of the risk of bloat. How long you wait is up to you.

## 3. Play

[Food and play training](/posts/food-and-play/) is how you build:

- **Bond.** You do fun stuff together.
- **Trust.** It's a safe way to learn something new.
- **Communication.** Your dog learns what your words mean.
- **Relationship.** Working with you becomes the best part of their day.

It doesn't take long. Five minutes a dog when you get out of the truck counts.

## 4. Dog jobs

Let your dog do what they were bred to do. Usually that means a safe, appropriate alternative.

| Bred to | Let them |
| -- | -- |
| Chase rabbits | Chase balls |
| Hunt herds of deer | Track lost people |
| Hunt mice | Sniff out hidden food |
| Bite | Play tug |
| Pull, like a husky | Go bikejoring |
| Herd | Work a frisbee |

**The easiest one is a scent game.** Hide a ball or some food and let your dog find it. You can work the nose every day, even with a puppy. Here's [Griz](/posts/griz/) learning detection at 13 weeks. Look how tiny she is!

## 5. Alone time

Dogs need a place to be alone. For mine, that's the kennel, and it's a safe place.

- **Good stuff happens there.** Food, bones, toys, whatever.
- **They come and go as they please,** most of the time. I close it when I need to.
- **They never get in trouble in there.** That's what keeps it safe.

That's also where they go for dinner and the hour after.

## 6. Relax

My dogs relax when I do. I put them in an extended down with no rewards, just negative reinforcement if they need it. Do that at the same time and place and it becomes classical conditioning: this time, this place, we're off.

## 7. Chill

Chill is not the same as relax. Chill training teaches a dog to be chill even when they're hyped up on rewards.

| | Relax | Chill |
| -- | -- | -- |
| They start | Nothing going on | Hyped |
| I use | No rewards, light pressure if needed | Food or play |
| They learn | It's time to be off | I can be excited and still settle |

Here's chill training in my shed while I listen to an audiobook. I do it in the house during a movie, too, or by the fire when we have company and my dogs are acting a fool.

## 8. Handle stress

Stress is part of life, so teach your dog ways to deal with it. I think of it as two different things.

**Escape and avoidance: the dog has a say.** They have agency. They can make the pressure worse, or they can move with it and [escape or avoid it](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/).

**Stress management: the dog doesn't.** This thing is going to happen and you have to deal with it.

| They have a say | It's happening anyway |
| -- | -- |
| Leash pressure | Grooming |
| E-collar work | Being picked up |
| Forced fetch | Muzzle training |
| | The vet's office |
