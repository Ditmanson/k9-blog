## She changed how I train

When I started training her I was all about force free, only food and play. She's the one who taught me that has its limits.

- **Where I started:** [food and play](/posts/food-and-play/), and nothing else.
- **What I added:** negative reinforcement with the [e-collar](/posts/e-collar/), for a couple of years before she became the dog she is now.
- **What I taught much later:** [leash pressure](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/). That's a bit out of order. These days I teach it first.

The e-collar opened up a world of [enrichment](/posts/dogs-need-enrichment/) on many adventures. These days I can trust her with no collar. I still put it on around prey animals and traffic. I can call her off a rabbit with a verbal cue, but it's not worth the risk to me.

## The hard part

She has low food and play drive. I've worked on it, but she's a rescue, and she came with some trauma that I can't seem to crack.

She's a really good demo dog for dogs with:

- **Low drive**
- **Low stress tolerance**

## Great with kids

She's really good for teaching kids how to use a leash. She responds so well to light pressure that my 6 year old, Cassius, can handle her on any trail.

## Back to school

Her training could be done. She's older, and she's well-mannered in every situation. But she got jealous of [Griz's](/posts/griz/) training and she asks for more, so I keep giving it to her. Lately we've been working on force fetch [stress training](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/), and I'll post it to her training records when we're done.

She crashes a lot of Griz's sessions, too. Here she is on a full lure for a sendaway, a drill she didn't know. She's learned from me before and understands luring pretty well, so she makes it look a little easier than it is with a green dog.
