A prong collar, also called a pinch collar, looks mean. It's also less likely to injure your dog than a [flat collar](/posts/flat-collar/), because the pressure is spread around the neck instead of landing on the throat. I use a Herm Sprenger.

## What it's for

A prong collar is used in conjunction with a leash. It provides the most responsive pressure with the least amount of pressure. I use it to teach negative reinforcement with light pressure.

Using it as positive punishment is a reactive response that we try to avoid. Corrections are for safety and/or clarity. [Have a plan](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/#have-a-plan).

The corrections aren't heavy. I get a good grip on the leash, not the loose stuff, and give just little ones.

## Try it on your leg

Put it around your leg and give it a nice tight yank. You'll realize the idea that it's going to pierce your dog's skin and cause lots of harm is ridiculous.

## Get the size right

What we really want: the martingale shouldn't hang wide open when it's resting on the neck, and it shouldn't close all the way during use. A good rule of thumb is as tight as you can go while it can still spin.

- **Sizing:** pinch the links together to take one out or put one back in.
- **Placement:** it goes up high around the neck, in front of all the other collars.
- **Too tight:** it won't spin, and it starts to bite earlier.
- **Too loose:** it can still spin even when it's tightened all the way. And if two dogs get in a fight, their collars can get hooked together. You'd probably be in for a pretty bad day.

## Live ring, dead ring

When the live ring is pulled tight, it closes the martingale and provides pressure. Attach the dead ring as well, and the martingale can't close all the way, so it acts similar to a flat collar. I don't really ever use it that way. I'd rather grab another collar, but if it's all I brought to a track, I might.

## The short tab

I often keep a short little leash on it for emergencies. When I'm walking and need to deal with something quick, it's right there.
