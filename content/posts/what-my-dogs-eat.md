You can feed your dog kibble. A lot of people do, and their dogs live long, full lives. I had one amazing dog who couldn't digest kibble, and that sent us down a long road of raw food. And you can't convince me kibble is better for them. Look at the price, then look at the ingredient list. You can't feed a living creature for that little money.

## How we got here

We've done it a lot of ways. We used to save money by getting livers, hearts, kidneys and the rest from farmers. That was gross for me and gross for the dogs. Yeah, dogs don't like the way organs taste. I've checked with about 5 dogs, and 5 out of 5 didn't like them. They'd eat it if they were hungry enough, but they'd pass if they weren't starving. I don't do organs anymore.

Then I moved to premade raw grinds for a while, and that was OK. But if you watch documentaries, even wolves eat berries, grass, and other non-meat stuff, and kibble already has fruit, veggies, and supplements. So we went looking for non-meat ways to get the micronutrients. You can make your own and save money, but I lack time, so I use Dr. Harvey's.

When money was tighter, I fed 50/50 raw and kibble. Things are good now, so we're cutting kibble out. I still give kibble as training treats pretty often.

## Dinner

Both dogs eat once a day, at dinner. The instructions say to mix Dr. Harvey's with hot water, meat, and oil. If I do that, Denali won't eat it, so I mix hers with an egg instead of hot water. Griz gets hot water and meat. She'll eat anything. I change proteins when I can, and when I can afford it.

## How much

I keep my dogs at the weight that lets them work best. If they get overweight or slow down, they get less. Griz gets more when she starts eating stuff she's not supposed to, like sticks, poop, and rocks. Denali's at the weight the vet calls perfect for her. Gaining or losing would be worse than where she's at.

How often depends on age:

| Dog | Meals |
| -- | -- |
| Before teething | 3 a day |
| After teething | 2 a day |
| Mostly full grown | 1 a day |

## Griz works for it

Most of the time, when I don't want to do much, Griz earns her dinner searching for the scent boxes. Sometimes we go outside and track. Sometimes we work positions or heeling, and I cut the meat up smaller so we can do different stuff with it. More on [dog jobs](/posts/dogs-need-enrichment/#4-dog-jobs).

## Treats

They get all kinds of treats, and I usually share what I'm eating. Griz gets most of the table scraps to beef up on calories. If we have something super healthy like salmon and broccoli, both dogs get some, and I cut some of Denali's food ahead of time.
