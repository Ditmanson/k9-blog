Griz's primary collar, the one with her tags on it, is a slip collar my wife made. I use it all the time, and it doubles as an 18" slip lead. Some people call them chokers, other people call them slip collars. She wears one because she's already confident with leash pressure.

## What it's for

It's useful for the same reasons as a [prong collar](/posts/prong-collar/). A slip collar is used in conjunction with a leash, and I use it to teach negative reinforcement with light pressure.

Using it as positive punishment is a reactive response that we try to avoid. Corrections are for safety and/or clarity. [Have a plan](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/#have-a-plan).

## The upside

- **Convenience.** It cinches up and doubles as a bit of a leash.
- **The look.** The public tends to like how they look better, since you see them at the vet's office all the time.

## The downside

- **More pressure.** It takes more pressure than a prong collar to get the same effect.
- **The throat.** It's better than a [flat collar](/posts/flat-collar/), because it tightens around the whole head instead of putting all the pressure on the throat. But it tightens in a way that's more dangerous to the throat than a prong.

## What I recommend

I'd always recommend a [prong, aka training collar](/posts/prong-collar/) over this. A slip collar is convenient after your dog is confident with [leash pressure](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/).

## Fit

For the best use, put it up high, by the ears.
