Flat collars are the most common collar out there. They're not for leash pressure, but they're not exactly useless.

## What it's for

- **Tracking.** I run the long lead between her legs, so when she pulls into it, the pressure lands on the top of her neck instead of the throat. If she goes too fast, all it does is pull her nose closer to the ground. That's why I use it for footstep tracking. I never make aversive corrections on the track.
- **Telling her what job is next.** I use flat collars and fur savers the same way: to classically condition a dog to a job. This collar on means we're doing air scent training. Her fur saver means tracking, not trailing. It's required for IGP tracking.
- **Tying up.** If I need to go in a store, a flat collar can hold her in place.

## The upside

- **Tags.** It holds a tag well.
- **The look.** It looks nice.

## The downside

When you pull on a flat collar, all the pressure comes right up on the front of the throat. For leash pressure, use a [prong collar](/posts/prong-collar/).

Some flat collars have a martingale strap, a section that tightens up as it gets pulled. That helps blockhead dogs, like Rottweilers and Cane Corsos, with big necks and little heads. When people tie them up, a plain collar can just slip off the head.

Denali's flat collar is a little wider. That's just for looks. It's nicer on her throat and it's prettier, but it doesn't do anything different.
