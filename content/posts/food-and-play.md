For most behaviors, I say start with food and play. You pay the dog with food or a game for what you like, and take away something they want for what you don't, usually by kenneling them.

It takes the novelty out of a behavior, lets you shape it without stress, and it's pretty much foolproof. By foolproof I mean this: even if you don't do it just right, you don't have to worry about creating trauma or making behaviors worse.

That's how Griz and I play: turns, heel positions, parade, center, boost, and backing up. To her it's all a game.

## What it's great for

- **Your relationship.** Working with you becomes the best part of your dog's day.
- **Precise movement.** Nothing teaches a focused heel better.
- **Recall.** Games build speed, and a dog that wants to come to you.
- **Drive.** Say *yes*, then pay. Do it enough and the word itself gets your dog fired up.

## Where it falls short

It raises your dog's energy, so if you want a calmer dog it might not be the tool for the job. On its own, you're also competing with the environment, and it doesn't teach a dog to handle stress. I have trained a dog with only food and play. It just takes more management than pairing it with [escape and avoidance](https://k9-communication.com/services/escape-and-avoidance-training/).
