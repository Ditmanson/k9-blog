## The two corners

All training comes from the four corners of operant conditioning. Food and play covers two of them: positive reinforcement and negative punishment.

{{< quadrant focus="ff" >}}

## Why I start here

- **It builds the relationship.**
- **It gets your dog addicted to training.**
- **It's fun.** For me, too.
- **It's [enrichment](/posts/dogs-need-enrichment/).** It's an appropriate outlet for their energy and instincts, and it's exercise for both of you.
- **It builds drive.** Working toward a goal runs on dopamine, which lifts their energy and makes them want more.
- **It takes the novelty out of an exercise** before we train it with [escape and avoidance](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/).
- **It softens the stress** of escape and avoidance, when you follow it up with some food and play. That's the Premack principle, aka [Granny's rule](#grannys-rule).

## It's foolproof

By foolproof I mean you can't cause any trauma to your dog. Doing it wrong doesn't hurt them, it just makes the behavior less clear. Most dogs will figure it out with enough reps anyway.

## Primary and secondary reinforcers

A reinforcer makes a behavior more likely to happen again. There are two kinds we should talk about.

| Primary | Secondary |
| -- | -- |
| Anything an animal needs to survive | The stuff that helps get them there |
| Food, water, shelter | Chasing, tug, nose work |
| My main one: **food** | My main one: **play** |

Many times play is the stronger drive, and the easier one to build on.

## Low play drive? Make them chase the food

You can encourage play with dynamic food rewarding. You offer the food, but your dog has to chase it down, because either:

- **You're moving**
- **You threw it**

## There's always some drive

Low drive isn't no drive. [Denali's](/posts/denali/) is low, and it's still there. There's always some drive, and we can always increase it:

- **Mess with the [diet](/posts/what-my-dogs-eat/).**
- **Find better treats.**
- **Play with the food.**

## Day one

We start with luring and reward marking. That's classical conditioning of two words.

| I say | It means |
| -- | -- |
| *Goooood* | Keep going (continuation) |
| *Yessss* | Done, come get paid (termination) |

[Here are all the markers I use](https://k9-communication.com/faq/verbal-markers/).

We often pair that with negative punishment. If I'm using food to train a sit and they down, they get the *uh-uhhh* and I withhold the food. It works best when a behavior is first being learned, before we start variable reinforcement schedules.

## Commands with drive

Dogs don't understand English, but they can seem to when a word reliably predicts a reward. Commands are markers too. *Sit*, *down*, *place*, and *here* get their meaning from the markers your dog already knows.

Taught with food and play, your dog wants to do them.

## How the pay changes

Reinforcement schedules change throughout time.

1. **A brand new behavior.** Continuous rewards. I pay for every completed behavior.
2. **As I add negative reinforcement.** I start paying on intervals, or randomly.

## Take it into the world

1. **Pick what you want them to behave around.** Your daily walk, guests at the door, other dogs.
2. **Find the threshold.** That's the point where they can still perform the behavior.
3. **Stretch it** until the behavior is reliable for your life.

At first the distraction is probably just me. When your dog is ready, we move somewhere more stimulating.

## 1. Obedience

- **Basic:** *sit*, *down*, *place*, and *here*, with drive.
- **Advanced:** precise positions, position changes, and focus. It's the kind of movement food and play teaches best.

## 2. Recall

Recall is the most important skill a dog needs, so we don't wait until the end. We play recall games to build speed and excitement, so your dog wants to come to you. Then we [bulletproof it](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/#2-recall).

## 3. Heeling

Heeling is a ton of fun. We tend to think of it as hard work, but it isn't. It helps with reactivity, keeps your dog safe near traffic, charges your markers, and builds the bond. You can do it from room to room, before dinner, almost anywhere.

- Casual
- Contact
- Focused
- Running
- Riding a bike
- Driving a car

## 4. Settling

We add distance and duration to *place*, *down*, and *sit*, then fade the rewards, the lure, and the place box. The box is useful, but you won't always have one in the real world. Here's [chill training](/posts/dogs-need-enrichment/#7-chill): hyped on rewards, and still settling.

## Granny's rule

Eat your vegetables, then you get dessert. For dogs, escape and avoidance is the vegetables and food and play is dessert. Do the stressful thing, then go play.

## Where it falls short

- **It raises energy.** If you want a calmer dog, it might not be the tool for the job.
- **You compete with the environment.**
- **It doesn't teach a dog to handle stress** on its own.

I have trained a dog with only food and play: [Everest](/posts/denali/#her-crews), my old German Shepherd. He passed his AKC Canine Good Citizen test in 2020. It can be done, it just takes more management than pairing it with [escape and avoidance](/posts/escape-and-avoidance/).
